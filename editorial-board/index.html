3-Tier Editorial Governance Model

A self-sustaining academic journal and student-run media house structure designed to survive Ph.D. scholar turnover.

Why a 3-Tier Governance Structure?

The greatest challenge faced by university initiatives is losing momentum when student leaders and Ph.D. scholars complete their research and graduate. KTUJM Scholars addresses this systemic turnover by adopting an institutional model inspired by peer-reviewed academic journals: permanent patron oversight, an annual rotational managing editor, and department-level scholar coordinators bound by mandatory handover rules.

The 3 Tiers of Responsibility

Tier 1: Founder & Permanent Patron

Permanent Oversight

Held by: Amir Hashmi (Alumnus & Ph.D. Scholar)

  • Maintains the long-term vision, hosting infrastructure, and institutional neutrality of the platform.
  • Oversees senior editorial appointments and approves the annual Managing Editor selection.
  • Leads major alumni outreach, high-profile interviews, and media partnership initiatives.
  • Acts as the permanent steward ensuring the platform remains non-commercial, advertisement-free, and student-focused.

Tier 2: Managing Editor

1-Year Rotational Term

Role: Senior Ph.D. Scholar (Current term: 2026–2027)

  • Maintains the active editorial publishing calendar and coordinates weekly submission reviews.
  • Acts as the operational bridge between the Founder and the campus student body.
  • Enforces note quality standards, plagiarism screening, and proper citation formats before publishing.
  • Supervises the Departmental Coordinators and guides junior scholars in editorial verification.

Tier 3: Departmental Coordinators

1–2 Scholars per Department

Role: Active Ph.D. Scholars across all 9 University Academic Departments

  • Collect, curate, and review study notes, model question papers, and reading recommendations for their respective departments.
  • Verify curriculum adherence against official university ordinances and notify the board of any syllabus changes.
  • Nominate impressive undergraduate and postgraduate students for contributor credits and mentorship.
  • Identify distinguished alumni from their department to feature in our Career & Alumni spotlight series.

The Mandatory Succession & Shadowing Rule

Guaranteeing Continuity across Graduating Batches

To prevent knowledge loss when senior researchers complete their degrees:

The Shadowing Rule: Every Departmental Coordinator and Managing Editor must recruit, mentor, and actively shadow a junior Ph.D. scholar (1st or 2nd year) for at least three months prior to submitting their doctoral thesis. The junior scholar participates in editorial meetings, manages git pull requests, and learns the verification protocol to ensure a seamless handover upon the senior scholar's graduation.

Contributor Recognition & Academic Credit Policy

Academic recognition is central to our open-access philosophy. Every verified note, article, or interview published on this platform permanently carries:

  • Student Contributor: Name, enrollment batch, and academic department.
  • Ph.D. Scholar Verifier: Name, research specialization, and official editorial credit.
  • Revision History: Timestamp and version notes tracked transparently via GitHub commit history.

Scholars and student editors receive an official certificate of editorial service and public attribution suitable for their academic curricula vitae (CV).
